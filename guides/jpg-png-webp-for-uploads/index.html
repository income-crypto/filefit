JPG vs PNG vs WebP: Which Format Should You Upload?

Choose the right image format for an application form, screenshot, signature or website—and avoid conversion mistakes that lead to rejected uploads.

✓ Free browser tools✓ Your files stay on your device✓ No signup

The short answer: follow the destination's accepted formats

The image that looks best on your screen is not always the format an online form accepts. A recruitment portal asking for JPG usually won't accept WebP just because WebP is smaller. Likewise, a screenshot with tiny text may be easier to read in PNG than in a heavily compressed JPG. Compatibility comes first; quality and file size come next.

JPG, PNG and WebP at a glance

FormatUsually best forKey trade-off
JPG / JPEGPhotographs, many application uploadsLossy compression and no transparency; thin text edges can suffer
PNGScreenshots, sharp text, logos, transparent imagesLossless image storage may produce larger files for photos
WebPWebsite images where the destination supports itOften efficient, but some application portals do not accept it

Choose by what you're uploading

Passport-style photographs

If the application says JPG/JPEG, use JPG and choose a quality setting that keeps facial details clear. PNG and WebP are alternatives only if the portal explicitly allows them. After converting, check its separate KB limit, pixel dimensions, background and crop rules. A change of format does not automatically satisfy all upload requirements.

Signatures and documents with thin text

PNG can preserve crisp edges, especially when the source is a clean scan or screenshot. If the portal requires JPG, select JPG and inspect the exported strokes closely. Repeatedly converting a compressed signature between formats can make fine lines look worse, not better.

Screenshots and UI images

PNG is a strong default when small labels, icons and hard-edged shapes must stay readable. WebP can be helpful for delivery on a website, but only where supported. If you're preparing a screenshot for a PDF, compare the final PDF's readability before choosing the smaller file.

Transparent logos or cut-outs

PNG and WebP can preserve transparency; JPG cannot. Converting a transparent image to JPG requires filling previously transparent pixels with a background. Check that the output looks right on white and dark surfaces and that the conversion did not turn the background into an unintended color.

How to convert without surprises

  1. Read the form's allowed file extension and format. If it says only JPEG, choose JPEG.
  2. Open Filefit's free JPG, PNG and WebP converter and choose the source image.
  3. Select the target image format. For JPG and WebP, the tool offers quality options; start with balanced or high quality when legibility matters.
  4. Download the result and open the new file. Check readable text, edges, transparency and color.
  5. Compare the new file's KB size and pixels with the destination's requirements. Use the KB compressor or pixel resizer only if needed, then check the result again.

Common myths that cause failed uploads

Can I just rename photo.png to photo.jpg?

No. File extensions are labels. Renaming a PNG file doesn't rewrite its internal image data into JPEG format. Some applications detect the actual content and reject a mislabeled file. Use a converter that creates a real JPEG.

Does PNG always have better quality than JPG?

PNG saves image information losslessly, but it cannot restore detail already lost in a prior JPG. Starting from a heavily compressed photo and saving it as PNG often just makes a bigger file with the same visible flaws.

Is WebP always the smallest file?

No. Results vary with image content, settings and dimensions. Try comparable quality settings and inspect the actual exported files. Compatibility may matter more than a few KB of savings.

Does a smaller KB size mean smaller pixel dimensions?

Not necessarily. Encoding can reduce bytes without changing width or height, while more aggressive compression may also resize. Check both measurements separately. Our troubleshooting guide explains common form-upload errors.
